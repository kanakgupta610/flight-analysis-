02 — The problem

The problem

Weather conditions such as heavy rain, fog, strong winds and thunderstorms can disrupt flight operations. Our investigation asks three questions:

Question 01

Does the probability of a delay differ across weather conditions?

Question 02

How likely is a flight to be delayed when a specific weather condition occurs?

Question 03

Is there a relationship between numerical weather measurements and delay duration?

Objective: use historical flight and weather data, conditional probability, and R visualisations to investigate these questions.

04 — The mathematics

The mathematical foundation

We represent flight and weather information using two random variables:

X

the weather condition

Y

the flight delay status

Joint probability — two events occur together
P(X = x, Y = y)
Conditional probability — one event when another is already known
P(Y | X) = P(X, Y) P(X)
for P(X) > 0

In simple words: we divide the number of delayed flights under a particular weather condition by the total number of flights under that condition.

Connection to Unit III: an application of joint and conditional distributions. The formula also applies to discrete variables such as weather category and delay status.

Rainy ∩ Delayed
P(D | R) = n(Rainy ∩ Delayed) n(Rainy)

07 — Visualising

Visualising weather and delays

A scatter plot helps us investigate the relationship between two numerical variables.

  • X-axis: wind speed (km/h)
  • Y-axis: flight delay duration (minutes)
  • Each point: one flight observation

If higher wind speeds tend to occur alongside longer delays, the points show an upward pattern. If they are widely scattered, the relationship may be weak or inconsistent.

plot(
  flights$wind_speed,
  flights$delay_minutes,
  main = "Wind Speed vs Flight Delay",
  xlab = "Wind Speed (km/h)",
  ylab = "Delay Duration (minutes)",
  pch = 19, col = "steelblue"
)

The final approach
